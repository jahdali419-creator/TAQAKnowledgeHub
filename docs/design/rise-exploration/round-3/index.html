G

Concept G · Knowledge as an operating system

RISE Command

Two more search states

Design thesis

RISE has no home page: home is a command surface that asks “What do you need?” and carries your context — your area, the active alert, the last document you opened. Search, area and document are the same bar with a scope, and every result shows why it matched, what was hidden and what you may open.

Signature interaction

Lanes and peek. Type, and results arrive in typed lanes (Procedures, Work Instructions, Standards, Lessons, Alerts, Experts, Areas, Glossary, proposed Equipment), each tagged with why it matched. Arrow keys move, Space opens a live preview with the trust verdict beside the results, and filters are tokens typed into the bar (status:current, area:coiled-tubing).

Assumption it challenges

That a knowledge platform is a website of pages you navigate. Here you state a need; navigation becomes a trail you can step back through.

Why it could win

  • One surface for every need: keyboard-first on desktop, thumb-first on the phone.
  • Radical honesty: it says “No approved document is titled for pump isolation” instead of guessing.
  • Scales to 520 documents and 25 areas without adding menus.

What could fail

  • Field users who never touch a keyboard may miss the power features.
  • The dark, OS-like look can read as a developer tool; it needs a light theme.
  • Ranking rules and the synonym table need governance to stay trusted.
Difficulty to implementMedium

Search, filters and role rules exist; the new work is lanes, match reasons, token parsing and a preview panel.

H

Concept H · Knowledge as a place

RISE Knowledge Map

Two more search states

Design thesis

TAQA’s knowledge is a place, not a list. Home is a calm map of all 25 areas in their four families, each sized by its real document count; zooming changes meaning, from area to equipment to the document and everything around it. On the phone the same idea becomes a vertical descent, and every map has a list beside it.

Signature interaction

Semantic zoom with an altitude gauge. Zooming into Coiled Tubing turns it into its equipment, each carrying its documents, with a “Pressure control” thread running out to Wireline and Slickline. A five-level gauge (TAQA › Grouping › Area › Equipment › Document) always shows how deep you are and takes you back up.

Assumption it challenges

That knowledge is lists and folders. Search shows where knowledge lives, and a document is a node with its equipment, alert, expert and lineage drawn around it.

Why it could win

  • Shows at a glance where knowledge is, where it is thin and where alerts sit.
  • Makes cross-area knowledge visible (pressure control across three segments) without searching.
  • Never traps anyone: a Map/List toggle and the trust verdict are always on screen.

What could fail

  • Equipment tags and topic threads don’t exist yet; the map is only as good as that curation.
  • A much larger register would need clustering rules to stay calm.
  • Some people may treat the map as decoration and go straight to search.
Difficulty to implementHigh

The landscape is easy; semantic zoom plus curating equipment tags and topic threads is real product and data work.

Concept I · Knowledge from where the work is

RISE Field Mode

Two more search states

Design thesis

RISE starts from the person standing at the equipment. Home asks one question, “What’s in front of you?”, and answers it three ways: scan, equipment or type. Whatever you open, a full-width Trust Card comes first, so in two seconds you know whether the document is safe to use.

Signature interaction

The two-second Trust Card. Scan, tap or search, and a huge verdict appears first: CURRENT with a tick, “In force. Safe to use on the job.”, revision, effective date, approver, what changed and the linked alert. Scanning an old print shows an unmistakable SUPERSEDED stop card with one button to Rev 4.0.

Assumption it challenges

That knowledge is navigated by organisation chart (area, shelf, list). Field Mode navigates by what is physically in front of you and puts trust before content.

Why it could win

  • Answers the field’s real question — can I use this? — in two seconds, in sunlight, with gloves.
  • Equipment and QR entry remove most decisions; nobody needs to know the numbering system.
  • Offline is designed in: status on every screen, pinned documents and crew job packs.

What could fail

  • Equipment tags and asset QR codes don’t exist yet.
  • Big type shows fewer documents per screen; office and QMS users will want denser views.
  • Job packs and asset labelling add operational process beyond the software.
Difficulty to implementMedium

Trust data, Pin offline and document QR codes already exist in the live app; equipment tags, camera scan, job packs and a Sunlight theme are new.
