The three directions

J

Clean Enterprise Product

Left sidebar

Design philosophy

The interface steps back so the documents are the content. Near-white surfaces, hairlines instead of cards and precise typography; TAQA Teal marks only where you are and what you can do.

Best feature

Search is the fastest to scan: a slim filter column with real counts and a clean results table grouped by type, with your area first.

Main weakness

It is the quietest of the three, so TAQA identity rests on the logo, BW Gradual titles and the teal accent and could drift towards generic SaaS.

Best suited for

Everyday use by the whole workforce, from technicians to QMS controllers, who need to find, check and open the right document quickly.

K

Technical Operations Hub

Top bar + area tree

Design philosophy

RISE as the document-control desk for operations: every screen leads with your area, the state of the register and the trust facts of each document. Dense register tables on desktop, calm single-column lists on the phone, and colour used only for state.

Best feature

The document-control band on WI-003 answers “is this the right, current document?” — status, revision, effective, next review, owner, approver, classification and the ALT-002 link — before the preview even loads.

Main weakness

The density and full document numbers are powerful for daily users but can feel heavy for an occasional reader.

Best suited for

Field supervisors, engineers and QMS users who check revisions, alerts and approvals every day and compare documents across areas.

Visual maturity8
Usability8
TAQA / RISE identity8
L

Editorial Knowledge Platform

Top navigation

Design philosophy

RISE as a quiet, authoritative publication: every page is composed like a section front, not a dashboard. Large light display type, strong document titles, cover-sheet previews and status stated in plain sentences.

Best feature

Home leads with what matters today — the active alert and the exact section it changes — and the document page pairs a large cover with “Current: this is the revision to use”.

Main weakness

Editorial spacing makes pages longer, so users who want many rows on screen scroll more than in a table-first design.

Best suited for

Supervisors, engineers and managers who need to understand what changed and trust the document they open.
