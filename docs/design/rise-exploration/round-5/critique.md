# Critique of round 4 (J, K, L) — why they are not enough

**Too similar.** On the phone, all three share the same skeleton: logo top-left, bell top-right, a five-icon
tab bar with the same icons, an off-white ground, and lists of rows where every row repeats
"code · area · status dot · Rev · classification · date". Swap the logos and most people could not tell
J, K and L apart. On desktop the differences (sidebar vs top bar) are structural but the rhythm,
component vocabulary and density of type are nearly identical.

**Stiff and stuffy.** Pages explain themselves instead of designing the answer: "2 not in force are
hidden", "Locked items: request access through QMS", "Coiled Tubing results first, because it is your
area" sit at the top of screens as grey sentences. Breadcrumbs, KPI strips ("36 · 2 · 1") and hairline
dividers everywhere give a register/form feel, not a product feel.

**Weak differentiation in layout and interaction.** No direction has a signature component or gesture.
Search is the same field + chip row + grouped list in all three; documents are the same header + metadata
grid + preview. Nothing is expressed through interaction (sheets, segmented controls, sticky actions,
progressive disclosure); everything is a long static column.

**Mobile not elegant enough.** Metadata is small and dense, rows are tall but cluttered, highlight
yellow on titles is loud, status, lock and Arabic badges compete on one line, and the 5-tab bars eat
space. The phone screens read as compressed desktop pages: too many borders, too few clear groups,
no breathing rhythm between sections, and little typographic contrast.

**Too much enterprise-portal energy.** Stat tiles, register tables, "View all" links, grey labels in
caps, and every feature visible at once. It looks like a well-organised intranet rather than a product
someone chooses to open.

**Not memorable for a global product.** The brand shows up as a logo and a teal accent only. There is
no confident type moment, no distinctive shape language, no recognisable composition. L came closest,
with its editorial type, but even L falls back on the same rows and chips on the phone.

**Not enough design confidence.** Each direction hedges: it shows everything, labels everything and
explains everything. World-class products choose. They lead with one clear thing per screen and let
the rest be one tap away.

## What round 5 must do differently
1. Design the phone first, screen by screen, with real typographic contrast and generous rhythm.
2. One clear job per screen region; move secondary facts into sheets, segmented views or disclosure.
3. Give each direction its own navigation model, component language and rhythm, not just a palette.
4. Make trust visual and immediate (status and revision as designed objects, not grey text).
5. Fewer borders, fewer badges, fewer explanatory sentences; more space, larger type, clearer groups.
