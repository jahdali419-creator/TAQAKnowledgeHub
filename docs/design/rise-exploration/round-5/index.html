Round 5 · Mobile first

Three product directions for RISE, designed for the phone first

M, N and O start from the phone and aim for the clarity, polish and confidence of a modern global product. Each has its own navigation model, rhythm and component language, and all three show the same real RISE content to the same Employee.

M

Direction M

Smooth Product Experience

Floating capsule tab bar · floating navigation panel on desktop

Design philosophy

A calm, carefully made app: soft tinted ground, large rounded surfaces and one clear job per region, with the rest one tap away. Trust appears as designed objects (a “Current · Rev 4.0” capsule and an approval card), not grey sentences.

What is better than the previous round

  • Each phone screen leads with one thing: a greeting and search, the best match, the area and its alerts, or the trust card.
  • Interaction carries the structure: floating tab bar, segmented controls and a floating action bar on the document.
  • Rows hold a title and one short line; access rules are said once instead of on every row.
  • Real type contrast (large display titles, 17px body) and groups separated by space, not borders.

Best mobile quality

The home first screen — greeting, search and a swipeable “For you” row with the active alert and WI-003 to continue — feels light, finished and easy to pick up.

Best desktop quality

A floating rounded navigation panel beside one large canvas gives desktop the same calm as the phone, with a three-card “For you” row and room to breathe.

Main weakness

Its grouped, rounded look follows familiar app conventions closely, so it is the most polished but the least distinctive of the three.

Direction N

Structured Technical Clarity

Persistent top search + 4-item bar · three-pane desktop

Design philosophy

A serious, calm instrument for people who need the right revision fast: crisp white, cool greys, teal for action and orange only for alerts. Every document gets an identity plate — number and revision set large, status as a signal with a shape, a word and a review-cycle gauge.

What is better than the previous round

  • Trust is visible in about two seconds: the identity plate, the green Current tile and a review-cycle bar with today marked.
  • Its own component: a stacked code column (SOP over 005) that makes lists scannable the way field users say numbers.
  • Search is always one tap away at the top of the phone; desktop becomes a real list-and-detail workspace.
  • Facts are labelled rows and dated timelines instead of metadata strings.

Best mobile quality

The WI-003 phone screen shows identity, revision, status, approver, review cycle and the ALT-002 §5 warning in the first view, with Open PDF one thumb-tap away.

Best desktop quality

Search becomes a three-pane workspace: results on the left, the selected document’s identity plate, record and preview on the right, without leaving the list.

Main weakness

It is the coolest and most information-rich of the three, so occasional readers may find it slightly dense next to M and O.

O

Direction O

Refined Editorial Utility

No tab bar · floating “Search RISE” pill · editorial masthead on desktop

Design philosophy

RISE reads like a calm, well-edited brief: each screen leads with one written answer, and everything else follows in a clear reading order. Documents are designed covers you recognise at a glance, and trust is said in one sentence instead of a metadata grid.

What is better than the previous round

  • No tab bar and no repeated row skeleton: home is a brief, the area is a contents page, the document is cover, trust sentence and reading index.
  • Change is shown, not described: the ALT-002 cover linked to WI-003 §5, and §5 marked in the reading index.
  • Secondary facts move into footnotes and disclosure; access rules and hidden results are said once.
  • The strongest type contrast of the three: large display headlines and light date numerals with a 17px reading body.

Best mobile quality

The home first screen is one idea, beautifully set: the headline, two covers linked by a thin rule to §5, one action, and search waiting at the thumb.

Best desktop quality

A centred editorial page with an asymmetric grid puts the brief beside “Your shelf” and your area, using the full width without a single dashboard tile.

Main weakness

Pages are long on the phone, and destinations like Ask an Expert and the Glossary sit in the menu rather than in a tab bar.

Why round 4 wasn’t enough

Too similar. On the phone J, K and L shared one skeleton: logo, bell, the same five-icon tab bar and rows repeating code, area, status, revision, classification and date.

Stiff. Screens explained themselves in grey sentences; breadcrumbs, stat strips and hairlines gave a form feel rather than a product feel.

No signature. Search and documents used the same components in all three, and nothing was carried by interaction.

Phone not elegant enough. Small, crowded metadata, loud highlights, competing badges and too many borders made the phone read as a compressed desktop.

Enterprise energy and low confidence. Everything was shown, labelled and explained at once; the brand was only a logo and a teal accent.

Scores are the design team’s first read, out of 10. No winner is chosen. Nothing is implemented in the live RISE application.
